The programme

How the Fast 500 is built

The ranking is compiled from applications to Deloitte’s national and regional Technology Fast 50 programmes across EMEA. Winners are determined by percentage revenue growth, nothing else.

1

Regional Fast 50 winners

Submitted automatically for Fast 500 consideration. This is how most of the 500 arrive.

2

Direct nominations

Accepted where no regional Fast 50 exists. This is the route open to Malta today.

3

Deloitte research

Deloitte also identifies public companies through its own research.

Sourcing routes as stated on Deloitte’s EMEA Fast 500 page (read via a search excerpt; the full page did not load for me). Confirm the direct-nomination mechanics with the EMEA team before relying on it.

Reading this for Maltese companies

  • Software, FinTech and iGaming-adjacent technology companies fit the sector list. Pure consultancies and resellers do not.
  • Deloitte states it assumes company-supplied information is accurate and does not independently verify it, so a clean audited-accounts trail protects both sides.

For Maltese companies

Why a Maltese company applies

🌍

International visibility

A place in a published EMEA ranking puts a Maltese company next to the region’s fastest growers, in front of investors, customers and partners outside Malta.

⚖️

An objective benchmark

Ranking rests on revenue growth alone. Deloitte Ireland describes it as an objective measure of performance, which gives third-party weight to a growth claim.

🔒

Revenue stays private

Programmes disclose the growth percentage, not current or base-year revenue. Revenue proof goes to Deloitte only.

🤝

Network access

Fast 50 programmes pitch access to Deloitte’s network of founders, funders and technology specialists.

🧾

Free to enter

No application fee in the programmes reviewed. The cost is the effort of assembling accounts.

🏁

First-mover position

With no Maltese entries on the map, the first ranked companies define how Malta is seen in this ranking.

For Deloitte Malta

Why Deloitte Malta organises it

The strategic case

  • Ownership of a national platform. Every other programme has a named Deloitte lead. Malta has none, so a Malta lead owns a vacant seat in the EMEA network.
  • A reason to call the right people. Applications create contact with founders and CFOs who would not take a cold approach.
  • Thought leadership with data. A published Malta ranking gives press, social content and proprietary market data, and complements the Technology Industry Survey in the FY27 plan.
  • Institutional goodwill. It gives a concrete topic for the planned meetings with the MFSA, MGA, MDIA and the Chambers.

Services high-growth clients need

Fast growers face enterprise due diligence: SOC 2 and ISAE 3402 attestation, AI Act readiness, DORA and NIS2 compliance, audit, tax and funding readiness. These match the FY27 action points.

Recurring, not one-off

Attestation and audit relationships renew annually, so a first engagement with a ranked company can compound.

The proposal

Replacing the forum with Fast 50

Proposed · Months 5–6
Malta Fast 50

A Deloitte Malta ranking of the 50 fastest-growing technology companies in Malta by percentage revenue growth, announced at an awards evening that takes the slot of the planned business forum. Ranked companies would be put forward for the EMEA Fast 500.

  1. Agree the route. Confirm with the EMEA Fast 500 team that Malta winners can be nominated directly.
  2. Set the criteria. Adopt the eligibility table above, with thresholds fixed for Malta.
  3. Open applications. Target the Tech.mt candidate pool and existing Deloitte tech clients.
  4. Verify and rank. Check revenue proof, then rank on growth alone.
  5. Host the evening. Announce the Fast 50, then nominate winners to the Fast 500.
Assumption to confirm. “Fast 50” follows the size of Deloitte’s national Fast 50 programmes. Whether Malta has enough eligible companies to fill 50 places is unconfirmed (the Tech.mt pool is 556 members, 248 with 6+ years), so a shorter list is possible. The size and the nomination route are not confirmed by Deloitte, and the Malta tie-in is a proposal.
